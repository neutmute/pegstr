Design Approaches

Three ways to build the sliding button cover rail system

A — One-piece rail frame

Single printed base with top & bottom channels. Cover inserts from the left end and slides right to cover button. Stopper tab at right end. Magnets embedded in base back face.

Pros

  • Sturdy, one piece
  • Self-aligning channels
  • Easy magnet placement

Cons

  • Cover must be loaded from left end

B — Two-piece split rail

Separate top and bottom rail pieces. Cover drops in between them. Each rail has its own magnet pockets. Stoppers built into both rail right ends.

Pros

  • Cover easy to remove/replace
  • Each rail thin — good for tight clearance

Cons

  • Must align two pieces independently on panel
  • Rails can drift apart over time

C — Closed frame (recommended)

Rectangular frame with top, bottom, and right walls. Cover slides in from the open left end. Right wall doubles as the stopper. Magnets in bottom bar. Cleanest look, most rigid.

Pros

  • Right wall is natural stopper — no extra part
  • Single piece, rigid frame
  • Clean appearance
  • Cover easily removed if needed

Cons

  • Frame must be slightly taller than button clearance
